# Marketing Context — 12-Section Template

> Reference loaded by `bmad-create-marketing-context` Phase 3 step 1 (Draft Marketing Context). This is the canonical 12-section structure every downstream BMAD marketing skill expects to find in `{marketing_artifacts}/marketing-context.md`.

## Why this template

Every workflow downstream of `bmad-create-marketing-context` (strategy, content, SEO, paid, CRO, lifecycle, RevOps, monetization, growth loops) loads `marketing-context.md` as its source of truth on product, ICP, positioning, and proof. If a section is missing, downstream agents fall back to assumptions and quality drops.

## The 12 sections (in order)

```markdown
# Marketing Context — [{company_name}]

_Generated by bmad-create-marketing-context on YYYY-MM-DD._
_Reload-friendly: every other workflow reads this file first._

## 1. Overview
One paragraph: what the product IS, what category it competes in, what business model
(SaaS subscription / freemium / usage-based / one-time / marketplace / hybrid),
launch year, current stage (pre-PMF / post-PMF / scaling).

## 2. Audience
Target companies: industry, size, region, headcount band, revenue band.
Decision makers: titles, seniority, departments.
Users: who actually opens the product daily.
Champions: who sells it internally.
Financial buyers: who signs the contract.

## 3. Personas
For each primary persona (max 3):
- Name + role + 1-line context
- Top 3 jobs to be done
- Top 3 frustrations with current alternatives
- Top 3 desired outcomes
- Where they hang out online (channels)
- What they read / listen to / watch
- Anti-pattern: who looks similar but is NOT this persona

## 4. Problems
The core pain the product addresses. Be specific:
- Who feels it (which persona)
- When they feel it (trigger event)
- What they currently do about it
- Why current solutions fail
- The cost of not solving it

## 5. Competitive landscape
- **Direct competitors** (3-7): what they do well, what they do badly, where you win.
- **Secondary competitors**: adjacent products users compare you to.
- **Indirect competitors**: alternatives users hire instead (spreadsheets, manual processes, doing nothing).
- **Differentiators**: 3-5 things only you do, with proof.

## 6. Differentiation
Three statements:
1. **Vs. direct competitors:** "Unlike [competitor], we [thing they cannot do], because [structural advantage]."
2. **Vs. indirect alternatives:** "Vs. doing it manually / vs. spreadsheets, we save [time / money / errors] by [mechanism]."
3. **Why now:** "[Trend / shift / new constraint] makes this product the right choice today."

## 7. Objections
Top 5-8 objections actually heard from prospects. For each:
- The objection in their words
- Whether it's true, partly true, or a misperception
- The response (acknowledge → reframe → proof)
- Asset that handles it (FAQ entry / case study / demo step)

## 8. Switching dynamics
Why users switch FROM something TO this product, and vice versa.
- **Push:** what makes them leave the old solution
- **Pull:** what makes the new solution attractive
- **Anxiety:** what scares them about switching
- **Habit:** what keeps them stuck on the old one
- **Trigger events:** what real-world events cause the switching consideration

## 9. Customer language
Verbatim phrases from real customer interviews / reviews / support tickets.
- Words to USE (theirs)
- Words to AVOID (jargon, internal terminology)
- Common spellings, abbreviations, plurals
- Tone calibration: formal? casual? technical? founder-voice?

## 10. Brand voice
- 3-5 voice attributes (e.g. "direct, opinionated, evidence-led, never preachy").
- Do say / don't say table.
- Tone shift across surfaces (blog vs. ad vs. support).
- Emoji policy.

## 11. Proof points
Stack-ranked by credibility:
- **Quantified outcomes:** "Customer X saved Y hours / $Z / W%"
- **Customer logos:** with permission, ranked by recognizability
- **Case studies:** title + outcome line + link
- **Testimonials:** 3-5 short quotes with name + role + company
- **Awards / certifications / press mentions:** dated
- **Public usage signals:** GitHub stars, PH upvotes, etc.

## 12. Goals & current metrics
- This quarter's primary business goal (signups / MRR / activation / retention).
- Current baseline metrics: traffic, signup rate, activation rate, MRR, churn, LTV, CAC.
- North-star metric and the 3-5 input metrics that move it.
- Knowable but not yet measured (gaps to fix in tracking).
```

## Quality bar

- Each section has at minimum 2-3 sentences. Empty placeholders are NOT acceptable — flag the gap and ask the user before publishing.
- Every claim in section 11 (Proof) must trace to a verifiable source (customer name, link, screenshot path).
- Section 9 (Customer language) needs at least 5 verbatim phrases, not paraphrases.
- Sections 4 (Problems) and 7 (Objections) must be specific to a persona; "users" is too vague.

## Versioning policy

- Save approved version as `marketing-context.md`.
- On re-run, archive the previous version as `marketing-context-v[N]-YYYY-MM-DD.md` before overwriting.
- A changelog comment at the top notes which sections changed.
